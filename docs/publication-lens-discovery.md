# Publication links and missing Harness guides

Discovery for [issue #30](https://github.com/ai-knowledge-hub/ai-harness-lab/issues/30), based on merged `dev` after [PR #35](https://github.com/ai-knowledge-hub/ai-harness-lab/pull/35). The scope and guide titles below are proposals for review. “Enterprise software” is a working name for the broader scope of the current Software & production track.

## Where a Publication link helps

A practitioner should be able to make an engineering decision using Harness alone. Link to a Publication section when it explains a trade-off that changes that decision. Link to a category when the reader wants to explore the wider subject. Keep one home for each guide and place the links beside the relevant material in References or Related reading.

Publication owns the category names and membership. A category can support several tracks, and a guide can have more than one useful category. For example, a guide to testing a product-description score could draw on Marketing for the definition of good creative, Growth for the business outcome, and Research for the strength of the evidence. Use whichever link answers the question at that point in the guide. Keep Publication categories out of Harness's track navigation.

## Three gaps to shape first

| Module | Current coverage | Proposed first guide and what the reader can produce | Other gaps to map after the first guide |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Enterprise software** | The lease queue lab and reporting collector explain retries, checkpoints, and partial results. | **Take a workflow from sandbox to production.** Use a scheduled internal report with sample data. Produce a system sketch, a release record naming the owner and permitted data, and a runbook that shows how to detect failure, recover, and stop or roll back the workflow. | Architecture choices across networks and services, integration contracts, identity and tenant isolation, data migrations, deployment, incident response, service targets, and cost at scale. |
| **Measurement & ML** | The lift guide explains credited conversions versus extra orders estimated by an experiment. | **Choose what evidence your decision needs.** Use an example where a team considers changing product copy after a favourable simulation. Produce an evidence plan that separates model evaluation, observed behaviour, and causal impact. Write the baseline and a rule for acting or waiting. | Metric definitions and data quality, experiment design, forecasting and classification against a baseline, threshold trade-offs and model monitoring, and optimisation under budget and feedback constraints. |
| **AI Engineering** | Start here distinguishes building with agents from building agent systems. The control plane, approval lab, and Skills links illustrate individual parts. | **Verify a small code change with an agent.** Use a fictional failure in a scheduled service. Produce a bounded task, independent verification criteria, and a handoff that reports checks and gaps. Use it to show the difference between checking one change and evaluating the agent across tasks. | Designing tools and permissions, state and recovery, coordinating agents and review, evaluating the system across tasks, and testing changes to prompts, tools, or memory before adoption. |

Keep these boundaries provisional. Enterprise software owns the operation of the service. AI Engineering owns the decisions about what an agent may do and how its behaviour is checked. Measurement & ML owns whether the evidence supports a decision. A shared worked case can link to all three without duplicating those guides.
